How it works

From the open web to a scored, traceable shortlist

TPDL's engine scans life-sciences companies for real commercial buying signals, scores each one on a fixed formula, and keeps every claim traceable to a source. Nothing here is a guess — a score exists only where dated, sourced evidence does.

The pipeline, step by step

1
Discover

Find candidate companies on the open web (life sciences: pharma, medtech, dental, diagnostics) — not just a fixed list, but new names surfacing in the news.

2
Tech scan

Detect each company's stack (CRM, analytics, marketing automation) — an absent CRM at scale is itself a signal of a commercial-infrastructure gap.

3
Research — 8 sources

Pull recent evidence from neural search (Exa ×3), financial press (Perplexity), Google News & Jobs (Serper), EU registries and investor pages.

4
Extract — verbatim

A first model isolates exact quotes from the raw text. It never judges or paraphrases — it only lifts the sentences that carry a signal.

5
Score — interpret

A second model reads only the isolated quotes (never the raw web) and scores relevance on the formula below. Separating the two is what makes it hard to hallucinate — the judge has no raw material to invent from.

6
Output

A scored, sourced row per company — summary, signals, links, tech stack — that lands in the Sales pipeline and the run history.

How a score is built

Deterministic — no black box. Computed per signal, then averaged over the signals actually found (never over all six categories).

score = signal strength (0–6) + recency (0–2) + corroboration (0–2)

Signal strength · 0–6

Commercial relevance of the signal — the only judgement the model makes.

Recency · 0–2

≤ 90 days = 2 · ≤ 6 months = 1 · no date = 0.

Corroboration · 0–2

2+ independent sources = 2 · one verified URL = 1 · unsourced = 0.

≥ 8 = outreach-eligible   the bar we act on. Below it, a company is monitored — and the Δ (change vs its previous score) surfaces the ones climbing toward 8: early good signs worth watching before they clear the bar.

The six signals we score

Each maps to where TPDL creates value. Product launches, certifications and undated claims are not signals — they're context, never scored.

Leadership change→Operating model · Commercial effectiveness
Hiring→Commercial effectiveness · Digital execution
M&A / expansion→Operating model alignment · CRM & data strategy
PE event→Operating model alignment
Digital initiative→Digital execution · Customer journey optimisation
Org restructuring→Operating model alignment · CRM & data strategy

Who's in scope (ICP)

The engine discovers broadly, then an ICP layer marks who's on-target. In scope: mid-size life-sciences brand owners (Europe, Middle East and beyond).

Flagged out of ICP (hidden by default, one click to show): consultancies, CDMO / CRO / pure manufacturing, and companies with known revenue under €100M. Private companies with undisclosed revenue are kept.

Runs & trajectory

Every scan is a dated run. A company scored again in a later run keeps its history, so you can read its trajectory over time.

Compare any two runs — or switch to “include runs between” to fold in every scan in the range and follow a company first-to-last across the middle (Recurring lays this out as a matrix).

Why you can trust a score
  • • Every claim traces to a specific source sentence and URL — open it and check.
  • • The model that judges never sees the raw web, only pre-isolated quotes — it can't invent facts.
  • • Unproven or undated signals are excluded, not scored.
  • • A QA pass flags anything thin for a 60-second human check before you act on it.
